Abstract

Reinforcement learning has become a practical language for modern LLM research: aligning models with preferences, improving reasoning, training agents through interaction, and optimizing multimodal systems. This introduction presents RL from the perspective of LLMs rather than robotics or classical control, using prompts, tokens, rewards, verifiers, preference data, and trajectories as the central objects.

The goal of this site is to make the paper easier to read and revisit. Readers can start with the online chapters, use the formula notes as a quick reference, and browse systems and datasets connected to the paper's discussion.

Chapters

  1. Introduction

    Motivates why RL has become important for LLM alignment, reasoning, agents, and multimodal models.

  2. Preliminaries: LLMs and RL

    Maps agents, states, actions, policies, rewards, and trajectories to LLM training.

  3. RL Training for LLMs

    Builds the path from policy gradient to advantage estimation, importance sampling, and PPO.

  4. Improved Reinforcement Learning for LLMs

    Explains advanced reward construction, advantage estimation, efficient RL methods, and direct preference optimization.

  5. RL for Reasoning

    Connects verifiers, process rewards, search, iterative RL, large-scale RL, and on-policy distillation.

  6. Agentic Reinforcement Learning

    Studies planning, tool use, environment design, credit assignment, memory, skills, and refinement.

  7. Multimodal Reinforcement Learning

    Introduces visual reward models and RL for multimodal understanding and generation.

  8. Conclusions and Future Directions

    Summarizes the paper and highlights RL for pre-training, efficient training, scaling limits, and continual self-evolving RL.
